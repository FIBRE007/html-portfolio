Your Expectation Can Be Bigger Than Your Ability

When God Is the Basis of What You Expect

← All articles

Have you ever believed God for something and then immediately started calculating whether you could make it happen?

You pray, but almost as soon as you finish praying, your mind begins taking inventory. How much money do I have? Do I know the right people? Am I qualified enough? Do I have enough experience? Where will the opportunity come from?

There is nothing wrong with asking practical questions. We should understand our circumstances, count the cost and make responsible decisions. But something happens when those questions become the final authority over what we expect. Without realizing it, we can begin with God and end with ourselves. We say we are trusting Him, but the size of our expectation is still being determined by the size of our ability.

Abraham had every natural reason to do that.

God had promised him a son, yet when Abraham looked at his circumstances, what he saw did not appear capable of producing what God had said. His body was old. Sarah was beyond the normal age of childbearing. The promise was not merely difficult; humanly speaking, the circumstances themselves seemed to argue against it.

And yet Romans 4 tells us something remarkable. Abraham did not have to pretend those circumstances did not exist. He could consider them without allowing them to become the final word. His confidence rested somewhere deeper: he became fully persuaded that what God had promised, He was also able to perform.

Scripture spotlight
Romans 4:18–21

Abraham considered the natural facts without allowing those facts to become greater than what God had said. His confidence was not in extraordinary human capacity, but in God's ability to perform what He had promised.

That changes the question.

Faith does not merely ask, “What can I accomplish with what I presently have?” Faith asks, “What has God given me reason to expect?”

That does not mean every desire that enters our minds automatically becomes something God has promised to fulfil. Faith is not permission to call our wishes God's promises. Our expectation needs a foundation in who God has revealed Himself to be, what He has said, and what He has made available to us in Christ.

But once God has genuinely given us a reason to believe, we must be careful not to reduce that expectation until it fits comfortably inside our present ability.

Paul prayed concerning the God who is “able to do exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think” (Ephesians 3:20). The emphasis is not on how extraordinary we can become. It is on how able God is.

This is what it means to say that God is our Source.

It does not mean that money does not matter, that employment does not matter, or that people, opportunities, skills and resources do not matter. They do. God often works through very ordinary means. A salary may meet your needs. A customer may sustain a business. Someone may introduce you to an opportunity. A particular job may provide the resources required for an important season of your life.

But none of these things is God.

They are means through which provision can come. We may call them channels. And understanding the difference between the Source and the channel changes how we respond when circumstances change.

A job can end. A customer can leave. An application can be rejected. Someone you expected to help may be unable to help. A plan can fail.

When we have unconsciously made the channel our source, losing the channel feels like losing the possibility itself.

But faith says, “The present channel has changed. My Source has not.”

Use them. Value them. Steward them. But do not ask a channel to carry the weight that belongs to God.

A short example

A woman has been searching for work for months. She has improved her CV, developed her skills, submitted applications, spoken to people and prayed. Then another rejection arrives.

The rejection tells her one thing: this particular application did not produce the job. Fear may add, “Nobody will employ me.” Condemnation may add, “Something must be wrong with me.” But those conclusions did not come from the rejection itself.

She can learn from the result, adjust her approach and continue knocking without allowing one closed channel to become a prophecy over her future.

That conviction does not make us passive. In fact, it gives us a healthier reason to act.

You can trust God for employment and still prepare your CV, develop your skills, submit applications and speak to people. You can believe God concerning a business and still study the market, manage your money, learn from mistakes and improve what you offer. You can pray for an opportunity and still knock on doors.

This is what participation means. It is simply doing responsibly what is within your hands while trusting God with what is beyond your hands.

You are neither saying, “Everything depends on me,” nor saying, “Because I trust God, I have nothing to do.”

You are saying, “I will faithfully do what I can because I trust the One who can do what I cannot.”

And perhaps that is where some of our expectations need to be corrected.

We have been looking at our bank accounts, qualifications, connections, experience and present opportunities and asking them to tell us what is possible. They can tell us what is presently available. They can tell us what we need to work on. They can help us make wise decisions. But they were never meant to tell us the limits of God.

So when what is before you appears bigger than you, do not immediately shrink your expectation until it becomes something your strength can explain.

Ask first, “What has God actually given me reason to believe?”

Then look at what is in your hands.

Pray. Think. Plan. Learn. Work. Ask. Knock. Adjust when necessary. Take the next responsible step.

Do responsibly what is within your hands while trusting God with what is beyond them.

What this does not mean

Does every desire become a promise?

No. Faith needs a basis in who God has revealed Himself to be, what He has said and what He has made available in Christ.

Does trusting God mean ignoring practical realities?

No. Faith can look honestly at circumstances without giving them final authority.

Does calling God my Source make jobs, people, medicine or money unimportant?

No. They can be valuable channels and should be used responsibly. They simply are not God.

Does faith remove my responsibility?

No. Grace gives us a different foundation for responsible action; it does not make participation unnecessary.

Bring the teaching home

Think about your own situation.

What are you presently expecting?

What has actually given you grounds for that expectation?

Which channel are you tempted to treat as your source?

What is presently within your hands to do?

Where does your ability genuinely end?

And when your ability reaches its boundary, remember that you have not necessarily reached God's.

Your expectation can be bigger than your present ability when your expectation is resting on someone bigger than you.

God is able.
